# flock 0.8.0

A simpler flock: one list of what you are running, one thing on screen at a
time, and a window that stays light with twenty agents in it.

## A calmer shell

- The sidebar is one list, after lucy-chat's: your projects and, under each,
  its threads — one task each, usually one agent, named after the task you
  gave it. A dot says what each needs: working, waiting on you, failed, or not
  started yet this launch. Projects you have not opened yet list their saved
  threads straight away; opening one starts its agents.
- A thread keeps the name its first task gave it, and once you hand its agent
  something else, says what the agent is on now on a quiet second line (your
  latest task, read from what you typed: no model call). Each agent's bar in a
  split says the same.
- The goose is redrawn: in flight, wing raised, a cream wing and breast on a
  body shaded in five blues, orange bill and feet. The wordmark is drawn on the
  same pixel grid, so bird and word are one mark, and the l's crest takes the
  bird's colours: the bill's peach and orange over the body's blues.
- "Waiting on you" sits at the top of the sidebar with a count, and ⌘J jumps to
  the next agent that is, in any project. ⌘⇧[ and ⌘⇧] move between threads.
- New agents start from a composer (⌘N): say what it should do, pick the
  project and the agent, press Enter. The thread is named after the task at
  once. Pick a folder there to start a new project. Branch strategies, secure
  mode and mixed lineups are under "More options" (⌘⇧N).
- Each thread has one header: its name (double-click to rename), project,
  branch, a ring for how full the agent's context is, the changes panel, add
  an agent, and one "…" for the rest.
- Settings, Graph, Pull requests, graph setup, your stats and a friend's are
  pages of the main panel, under the same head as a thread (its name, its
  views as quiet tabs, a close), instead of dialogs over the window. Opening
  one closes whichever was up; Escape or the close returns to the thread.
- The composer shows two chips: the project and the agent. The agent's model,
  effort and "More options" are one step into its menu, and the chip names a
  model or effort only once you pick one.
- The window's own buttons sit on the header row's centre line, and the
  sidebar toggle keeps one place, clear of them, whether the sidebar is open
  or hidden (Settings and every page carry it too).
- Your plans' usage sits at the foot of the sidebar: a thin bar per agent
  you are signed in to (Claude, Codex) for the window you are in. It keeps the
  ink until the limit is near, then turns amber or red and says when it
  resets; click it for the week, per model and credits. v1 drew it as an XP
  bar in the title bar.
- Paper and ink: white panels on a pale frame (or their dark twins), hairline
  rules, the system font, colour only for state. Terminals sit on the panel's
  own ground. Themes are Light, Dark and High contrast, following macOS until
  you pick one.
- Failures, finished merges, new pull requests and agents that need you in
  another thread arrive as short notes in the corner.

## Lighter on your Mac

- Only the thread on screen has live terminals. Other agents keep running at
  full speed; the backend keeps their output, status and start, and a thread's
  terminals are rebuilt from that output when you open it. v1 kept every
  terminal of every workspace mounted and streamed every byte of every agent
  into the window.
- Threads are named locally, the moment you give the task. v1 started a
  `claude -p` process (through a login shell) after every prompt to every
  agent to relabel it; v2 asks for at most one short AI title per thread, and
  Settings → Appearance turns that off.
- Git state is polled only for the project on screen, and less often. Saved
  output is written every 30 seconds instead of 15.
- Presence no longer retries forever when it cannot connect: one failure,
  then a try after 2, 4, 8, 16 and 30 minutes. While the presence service
  could not authorise (since 0.7.4x), Ably's own loop had been asking it again
  every 15 to 30 seconds.
- Agents no longer inherit the environment of whatever launched flock. A dev
  build started from an agent's terminal (or `open` from a Claude Code
  session) used to hand every agent that session's `CLAUDECODE` and
  `CLAUDE_CODE_*` variables, which turned Claude Code's transcripts off, and
  the launching pane's `FLOCK_*` identity.

## Friends and sessions

- People, under your projects in the sidebar (folded until you open it, its
  head still saying who is online and whether anyone is asking): friends with
  their running agent count, requests to accept or decline, adding someone by
  handle or inviting them by email.
- A friend's "…": watch their agent, co-pilot together, send a task. A
  co-pilot session is a temporary project with your agents and theirs; watching
  is a temporary project with their one agent, read-only. Their requests wait
  in the corner with an answer on them.
- Sharing reads a pane's output from the backend, so it keeps going while you
  look at another thread.
- Your stats, usage chart and achievements (your name, at the bottom of the
  sidebar, or ⌘K), and a friend's from their row.

The Graph has a row of its own in the sidebar: Knowledge, Map, Recall and
Insights are views of one page, or its setup when the graph is off.

## Removed

- Panes borrowed from another workspace into a tab.
- The titlebar notification pill and its log, the status bar, the right rail
  and dockable sidebar sections, quick actions and the feature tour.

The prompt queue stays: ⌘⇧P (or "Save prompt for later" on an agent) keeps a
prompt, and ⌘K sends it to the agent on screen.

## Security

- Dependencies updated for advisories published since 0.7.50, among them
  rustls and rustls-webpki (TLS), the bundled web root certificates, tokio
  and the frontend's build tooling. Crates that are flagged but never built
  into the Mac app (zip, and zbus's type crates on Linux) are recorded as
  such in `security/dependency-exceptions.json`, which the release gate
  re-checks on every run.

## Known limitations

- An agent that writes to the main screen rather than taking the whole
  terminal keeps the last 256 KB of its output as history when you come back
  to its thread; full-screen agents (Claude Code, opencode) repaint completely.
- Docker workspace isolation has a known Git-configuration escape: repository-controlled Git configuration can execute commands on the host. Do not rely on this boundary for untrusted repositories or agent code.
- Presence and sessions need the server side that 0.7.4x already expected
  and production never received: migration `013_social_privacy.sql` on the
  flock ID database, then the new presence-auth service, then the Ably
  settings in `services/flock-id/PRIVACY.md`. Until then friends show
  offline and sessions cannot start.
